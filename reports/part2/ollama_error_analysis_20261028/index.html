3. Some retrieved evidence missed the subject

79 of the 200 verifiable claims lacked a complete FEVER-annotated group in the retrieved five sentences. Llama answered NEI on 61 of those 79.

Coverage does not prove every unannotated alternative is insufficient. The Juventus case below is a concrete retrieval failure.

The title search used the first six non-stopword positions: juventus OR rejected OR their OR traditional OR black OR white. Its top ten title matches excluded Juventus_F.C., so none of that article’s sentences reached the sentence ranker.

Juventus F.C. rejected their traditional black-and-white-striped home uniform in 1903.

FEVER: REFUTES · Llama: NOT ENOUGH INFO

The evidence does not mention Juventus F.C. or their uniforms, so it does not support or contradict the claim.

Retrieval failure is visible here: none of the five texts concerns Juventus uniforms. The evidence-only NEI response is reasonable for the supplied texts, while FEVER expects REFUTES from the full Wikipedia annotation.

4. What happened when we supplied annotated evidence?

A separate 20-claim oracle diagnostic sampled cases with incomplete original annotation coverage, without selecting on whether Llama was right.

7 / 20 → 13 / 20 correct

Only the evidence changed to a complete FEVER-annotated group. 6 errors were fixed and 0 new errors appeared.

This is an artificial gold-evidence intervention on a selected subgroup, not a production result or an improved benchmark score. Relevance and evidence length/amount change together.

5. Some errors remain with annotated evidence present

In the original pilot, 36 of 121 verifiable claims with a complete annotated evidence group were still labeled NEI by Llama. Correct labels on this subgroup: Llama 73/121; combined system 90/121.

The Psych example shows an explicit date mismatch being accepted. Better retrieval alone does not explain all the errors.

Checks and interpretation

No invalid responses. All 300 responses completed. Maximum prompt plus generated tokens: 594 / 4096. All evidence fit fully in the matched RoBERTa 512-token input.

Strict FEVER moved differently from label accuracy: Llama 46.33% versus combined 44.00%. The paired interval for that strict-score difference includes zero.

These observations describe this local model, prompt and pilot. They do not establish that all LLMs perform worse or reveal a model's hidden reasoning.
