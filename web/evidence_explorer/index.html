TP / FP / TN / FN

What do these four outcomes mean?

For the selected positive label: TP = correctly predicted that label; FP = predicted it incorrectly; FN = missed it; TN = correctly predicted something else in this binary comparison. A TN may still confuse the other two FEVER classes. These counts are one-versus-rest, not a separate binary model.

These are observed label and annotation-coverage patterns, not proven causes. Open a claim to trace article search, sentence ranking, truncation, and the two models' decisions.

How is the evidence ranked?
01 / Find articles

Remove stopwords, use up to the first six remaining word positions, then search article titles with BM25. Keep up to 10 articles. Lower title BM25 scores rank first.

02 / Rank sentences

Each unique shared claim word earns one point. Add a small bonus for the article's rank. Higher sentence scores rank first.

score = shared words + 0.1 / article rankArticle rank starts at 1. Ties use article rank, then the original sentence ID.
03 / Select five

The classifier receives only the first five sentences. A word match is not an entailment judgment. FEVER annotations let us check whether a complete evidence group was found.

Where did the claims and this page come from?

The 19,998 claims and human reference labels come from the official FEVER development dataset. They were not generated by this viewer. This page was added with AI assistance to the research codebase; it reuses the project's existing retriever and saved RoBERTa predictions. It is an inspection interface, not a new trained model. Tokenization is replayed locally to inspect the input limit. Most of the original FEVER training pool was not evaluated by this 19,998-claim run.

This link works on the computer running the local server. A professor on another computer needs a packaged demonstration or a separately deployed version.
